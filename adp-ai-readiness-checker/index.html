How to work with your AI: what to weigh before you add it to your toolset

Adopting AI isn't a purchase — it's a redesign. Four questions decide whether new tools create value or quietly create more work.

What this comes down to

  • Rethink workflows — don't just automate what you already have.
  • Govern before you scale — ownership, aligned policy, open dialogue.
  • Build AI literacy across the whole workforce, not just tech.
  • Earn trust — transparency and human oversight on people decisions.

What good looks like

The eight questions, explained

Before you score yourself, here's what each question really asks — and why it decides whether AI creates value or more work.

1

Reimagine the work

Have you named the real problem — not just the tool?

Transformation starts with the outcome you're chasing — fewer errors, faster cycles, better decisions — not a product you want to buy. Shopping for a tool to fit a vague need is how AI quietly creates more work.

Have you redesigned the workflow end to end?

Real gains come from re-sequencing work around value: eliminate, combine or re-order steps, then embed AI where it strengthens human judgment — rather than bolting a chatbot onto today's approval steps.

2

Build governance

Does every AI decision have an owner and a review path?

Accountability is the foundation: name who owns each AI-assisted decision and how it gets reviewed. Gartner expects 40%+ of agentic AI projects to be canceled by 2027 — often because no one owned the risk.

Are your AI policies aligned across teams before rollout?

One shared standard beats team-by-team improvisation. Build compliance in by design, with audit trails and transparency — the way ADP governs use cases through an AI & Data Ethics Council.

3

Grow AI literacy

Do literacy pathways reach well beyond the technical team?

Adoption is only as strong as its weakest team. Extend AI literacy to operations, service, implementation and frontline roles, and frame it as a career enabler so it stays inclusive — not niche expertise.

Do you track literacy alongside adoption and value?

Measure literacy as a metric so skill gaps surface early, and give people a safe, governed assistant (like ADP Assist) instead of unmanaged tools on personal devices.

4

Earn trust

Do people know what data is used, how, and why?

Trust needs transparency: tell employees what data feeds the tool, how it works, and its benefits and risks — and make the resulting decisions explainable and auditable.

Is there a human-in-the-loop on people decisions?

Wherever an AI decision meaningfully affects a person, keep a human checkpoint. In HR that isn't optional — it's what turns “we built it” into “we built it together.”

AI Enablement Roadmap

The steps behind each readiness question · based on the ADP SPARK article “How to Work with Your AI” by Heather Bussing

How to use this. Every step is listed in full — nothing is pre-checked. Hold it against your self-check answers (Not at all / In part / In full) and decide, topic by topic, where to start and what can wait.

1 · Reimagine the work

Have you named the real problem — not just the tool?

  • Run a problem-framing session. Bring the stakeholders together and write down the outcome you're chasing — fewer errors, faster cycle time, better decisions — and the friction in today's process.
  • Write a one-page problem statement. State the measurable outcome and the constraints, and circulate it for sign-off so everyone is solving the same problem.
  • Shortlist tools only against that statement. Judge candidates by how well they serve the named outcome, not by their feature lists.

Have you redesigned the workflow end to end?

  • Map the workflow end to end. Document every handoff, approval and wait state before you choose anything.
  • Eliminate, combine or re-sequence. Remove steps that shouldn't exist so you don't automate waste.
  • Place AI where it strengthens judgment. Embed it at decision-support points, not as a chatbot bolted onto today's approval steps.

2 · Build governance

Does every AI decision have an owner and a review path?

  • Assign a named owner to each AI-assisted decision. Someone accountable for the outcome and the risk.
  • Document the review path. Define who reviews, when, and what triggers escalation to a human.
  • Keep an auditable log. Record decisions so they can be reviewed after the fact.

Are your AI policies aligned across teams before rollout?

  • Consolidate team rules into one AI policy. Replace team-by-team improvisation with a single shared standard.
  • Adopt compliance-by-design. Build audit trails and transparency into the workflow from day one; ADP models this through an AI & Data Ethics Council.
  • Set security and privacy controls. Protect employee data across every market you operate in.

3 · Grow AI literacy

Do literacy pathways reach well beyond the technical team?

  • Open pathways to every function. Reach operations, service, implementation and frontline roles, not just specialists.
  • Frame literacy as a career enabler. Position AI skills as growth for everyone so adoption stays inclusive.
  • Provide role-specific training. Teach people how the tools apply to their actual work.

Do you track literacy alongside adoption and value?

  • Define literacy metrics. Measure them next to adoption and value so gaps surface early.
  • Give a safe, governed assistant. Offer a managed tool (e.g. ADP Assist) instead of unmanaged LLMs on personal devices.
  • Review the metrics regularly. Use them to target training where it's needed.

4 · Earn trust

Do people know what data is used, how, and why?

  • Be transparent about data and purpose. Tell employees what data feeds the tool, how it works, and its benefits and risks.
  • Make decisions explainable. Ensure people can see why an AI-influenced outcome happened, and audit it.
  • Open a two-way channel. Create a real path for questions and concerns about AI at work.

Is there a human-in-the-loop on people decisions?

  • Identify decisions that affect people. Flag anywhere an AI output meaningfully affects a person.
  • Insert a human checkpoint. Keep a person in the loop to review and, where needed, override.
  • Build it with people, not at them. Frame AI with purpose so it's “we built it together.”
